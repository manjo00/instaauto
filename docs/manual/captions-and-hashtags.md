## The thing that changed (December 2025)

**Hashtags no longer expand reach. They classify.**

Instagram cut the limit from thirty to **five** and ended hashtag-following. With thirty
slots, tags were a distribution tactic; with five they are a filing label. The algorithm
now reads the caption, on-screen text, audio and the image directly.

The consequence that matters: **the caption is the discovery surface now.** Keyword-rich
captions measure ~30% more reach and roughly double the likes of hashtag-heavy posts.

So learning to write a caption is worth more than learning to pick hashtags — and it is
also the part that can sound like a person.

Verified against [Later](https://later.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-using-instagram-hashtags/),
[Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)
and [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-5-hashtag-limit-2026).
Enforced in `PublishPolicy.MAX_HASHTAGS`.

## Naming a piece

**Keywords:** title, name my art, what to call it, naming, untitled, piece name

Naming feels hard because the instinct is to *describe*. The image already did that. A
title's job is to add the thing the image cannot say.

Titles come from five places. When stuck, walk them in order:

1. **The feeling** — yours while making it, or theirs looking at it. *Restless.*
2. **One specific detail** — something small inside the frame. *The Third Window.*
3. **Time or place** — real or invented. *4am, Still Awake.*
4. **What it almost was** — the version painted over.
5. **Borrowed language** — a line from whatever was playing or being read at the time.

**The rule that fixes most of it: never use your first title.** Write three, then discard
any that merely describe the image. *"Sunset Over Mountains"* is a stock-library caption.
*"The Long Way Home"* is a title.

**Two shapes to avoid**, because both read as placeholders:
- colour + noun — *Blue Mountain*
- gerunds — *Flowing*, *Dreaming*

## Writing the caption

**Keywords:** caption, what to write, blank page, stuck, what do I say

The problem is usually not writing — it is the blank box at the moment the piece is
finished. Structure fixes that.

**A shape that works every time:**

1. **One line that isn't about the art.** A thought, a confession, a question.
   *"This one fought me for three weeks."*
2. **One concrete process detail.** What was hard, what changed, what nearly got abandoned.
3. **An invitation.** Something answerable in three words.

Why that order: people engage with the person, not the render — and the process is the only
part nobody else could have written. *"Here's my art"* is interchangeable; *"I repainted the
sky four times"* is not.

**The single habit that helps most: write the caption while working, not after.** Keep one
note per piece and jot the annoying thing that happened that day. By the time it is finished
the caption has written itself, and the blank box never happens.

*(That habit is what the "working notes" idea in ROADMAP would put in the app.)*

## Choosing five hashtags

**Keywords:** hashtags, tags, which hashtags, how many, five, tag strategy

Treat the five slots as a portfolio where each does a different job:

| Slots | Type | Aim for |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | **Niche community** — the community the work actually belongs to | 50K–500K posts |
| 2 | **Topic** — what the piece literally is | 100K–1M posts |
| 1 | **Flexible** — medium, format, or a personal signature tag | any size |

**Size is the whole game.** `#art` has ~2 billion posts — buried within seconds. Even
`#digitalart` is enormous. The 50K–500K band is where people actually browse and where a
post can surface.

**How to find your own five:** don't copy the big accounts. Find artists *at your size*
whose work looks like yours and who get real engagement, and look at what they use. Then
search each tag in Instagram — it shows the post count, and the top posts tell you
immediately whether your work belongs there.

**Three mistakes that cost reach:**
- **Mega-tags** — no useful classification signal.
- **The same five on every post** — reads as a spam pattern. This is why the app argues for
  several small themed sets rather than one big one.
- **Irrelevant trending tags** — actively misleads the classifier and works against you.

**First-comment placement buys nothing.** The five-tag cap spans caption and comments
together.
